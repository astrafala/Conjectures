\title{The empirical recurrence for OEIS A269637 is false at every index its own data
can test, and the correct one, of order 13}
\author{Adrian Perez Fontelles\\ \small Independent researcher}
\date{22 September 2026}
\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
OEIS A269637 carries an empirical recurrence of order $10$ contributed by R.~H.~Hardin.
It is false, and it is false in the strongest sense available: the entry publishes $20$
terms with offset $1$, so the recurrence asserts something at each of
$n=11,\dots,20$, and it fails at all ten. The first failure is at $n=11$, where the
entry's own DATA gives $199677806$ and the recurrence gives $199034092$. No model and no
computation beyond ten multiply--adds on the entry's published integers are needed for
that. The sequence is a transfer-matrix count, so it is $C$-finite and its true minimal
recurrence can be computed and proved; it has order $13$, and its first $10$ coefficients
are exactly the published ones. The published line is therefore the correct recurrence
with its last three terms dropped. Both statements are proved here in exact integer
arithmetic.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\small 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 05A15, 11B37, 15B36.\normalsize

\section{The sequence and the claim}

OEIS A269637 is ``Number of length-n 0..5 arrays with no repeated value differing from the
previous repeated value by other than plus two or minus 1.''. It has offset $1$ and its
published DATA is
\begin{gather*}
6,\; 36,\; 210,\; 1209,\; 6876,\; 38738,\; 216528,\; 1202353,\; 6639294,\; 36486190,\\
199677806,\; 1088813760,\; 5918190122,\; 32077034918,\; 173421804818,\; 935474530883,\\
5035906367464,\; 27059901888584,\; 145161762258568,\; 777537033208493
\end{gather*}
--- $20$ terms in all. The entry carries one formula line:
\begin{quote}\small
Empirical: a(n) = 29*a(n-1) -330*a(n-2) +1730*a(n-3) -2815*a(n-4)\\
-9981*a(n-5) +36088*a(n-6) +31192*a(n-7) -161403*a(n-8) -158561*a(n-9)\\
+291970*a(n-10)
\end{quote}
As of the live entry (revision $4$, last modified Mar 02 2016) this is recorded as
empirical and nothing on the entry records it as settled or as corrected. Written out,
the claim is
\begin{multline}\label{eq:conj}
a(n)\;=\;29\,a(n-1) - 330\,a(n-2) + 1730\,a(n-3) - 2815\,a(n-4) - 9981\,a(n-5)\\
{}+ 36088\,a(n-6) + 31192\,a(n-7) - 161403\,a(n-8) - 158561\,a(n-9) + 291970\,a(n-10),
\end{multline}
of order $10$, with no range stated and no initial index excluded.

With offset $1$ and order $10$, the first index at which \eqref{eq:conj} asserts anything
is $n=11$, and the entry publishes terms through $n=20$. Unlike the usual case, the claim
is therefore testable --- ten times over --- on the entry's own data, with no model
involved.

\begin{proposition}
The recurrence \eqref{eq:conj} is false. It fails at every one of the ten indices
$n=11,\dots,20$ at which the entry's published DATA can test it, the smallest being
$n=11$, where the two sides differ by $643714$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Direct evaluation in exact integer arithmetic, using only the twenty integers the entry
publishes. For $n=11$,
\begin{align*}
&29\cdot36486190 - 330\cdot6639294 + 1730\cdot1202353 - 2815\cdot216528 - 9981\cdot38738\\
&\quad{}+ 36088\cdot6876 + 31192\cdot1209 - 161403\cdot210 - 158561\cdot36 + 291970\cdot6\\
&\quad=\;199034092\;\ne\;199677806=a(11).\qedhere
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

Nothing about $n=11$ is exceptional; the discrepancy grows by a factor of between
$5.5$ and $6$ at each successive index, and the recurrence continues to fail for every
larger $n$ as well, as the corollary in Section~3 shows.

\section{A model, and the recurrence that does hold}

\subsection{What the entry counts}

Read the name as it is written. An array is a tuple
$(x_1,\dots,x_n)\in\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}^{n}$. Say that index $i\ge2$ \emph{repeats} when
$x_i=x_{i-1}$, and call $x_i$ the repeated value there. Listing the repeated values in
order of $i$ as $v_1,v_2,\dots$, the condition is that each differs from its predecessor
by plus two or minus one:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cond}
v_{j+1}-v_j\in\{+2,-1\}\qquad\text{for every } j\ge1,
\end{equation}
with $v_1$ unconstrained. (A run of three equal values contributes two repeated values
with $v_{j+1}-v_j=0$, so such runs are excluded by \eqref{eq:cond}; this is forced by the
data, see below.)

Let $a(n)$ be the number of such tuples. This reading was confirmed against the entry by
direct enumeration of all $6^{n}$ tuples for $n=1,\dots,7$, which gives
\[
6,\;36,\;210,\;1209,\;6876,\;38738,\;216528,
\]
the entry's first seven terms exactly.

\subsection{The transfer matrix}

Whether a tuple can be extended depends on the last entry and on the last repeated value
so far, and on nothing else. Take as vertex set
\[
\mathcal V=\{0,\dots,5\}\times\bigl(\{0,\dots,5\}\cup\{\varnothing\}\bigr),
\qquad |\mathcal V|=S=42,
\]
a vertex $(L,P)$ recording the last entry $L$ and the last repeated value $P$, with
$\varnothing$ meaning ``no repeat yet''. Define $M$ by: from $(L,P)$, appending
$x\ne L$ leads to $(x,P)$; appending $x=L$ leads to $(x,x)$ and is allowed only when
$P=\varnothing$ or $x-P\in\{+2,-1\}$. Let $\mathbf u\in\mathbf Z^{\mathcal V}$ be the
indicator of $\{(t,\varnothing):0\le t\le5\}$ and $\mathbf 1$ the all-ones vector. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model}
a(n)\;=\;\mathbf 1^{\!\top}M^{\,n-1}\mathbf u\qquad (n\ge1).
\end{equation}
Evaluated exactly, \eqref{eq:model} reproduces all $20$ terms of the entry's DATA and all
$210$ terms of the entry's b-file, and its first seven values agree with the brute-force
enumeration above.

\subsection{The true recurrence}

Being a walk count, $a$ is $C$-finite. Solving over $\mathbf Q$ for a
constant-coefficient recurrence from the model's own terms, the least order that admits
one is $13$, and the coefficients come out integral:
\begin{multline}\label{eq:true}
a(n)\;=\;29\,a(n-1) - 330\,a(n-2) + 1730\,a(n-3) - 2815\,a(n-4) - 9981\,a(n-5)\\
{}+ 36088\,a(n-6) + 31192\,a(n-7) - 161403\,a(n-8) - 158561\,a(n-9) + 291970\,a(n-10)\\
{}+ 583350\,a(n-11) + 364874\,a(n-12) + 80630\,a(n-13).
\end{multline}
The first $10$ coefficients of \eqref{eq:true} are, term for term, those of
\eqref{eq:conj}. The published line is \eqref{eq:true} with its last three terms,
$+583350\,a(n-11)+364874\,a(n-12)+80630\,a(n-13)$, missing.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:true}
\eqref{eq:true} holds for every $n\ge14$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let
\begin{align*}
p(t)\;=\;&t^{13}-29t^{12}+330t^{11}-1730t^{10}+2815t^{9}+9981t^{8}-36088t^{7}\\
&{}-31192t^{6}+161403t^{5}+158561t^{4}-291970t^{3}-583350t^{2}-364874t-80630
\end{align*}
be the characteristic polynomial of \eqref{eq:true}, and put $w=p(M)\mathbf u$. Formed by
thirteen matrix--vector products in exact integer arithmetic, $w$ is the zero vector of
$\mathbf Z^{42}$. Hence for every $n\ge14$,
\[
a(n)-\sum_{i=1}^{13}c_i\,a(n-i)\;=\;\mathbf 1^{\!\top}M^{\,n-14}p(M)\mathbf u
\;=\;\mathbf 1^{\!\top}M^{\,n-14}w\;=\;0 . \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The annihilation here is stronger than the usual Cayley--Hamilton argument needs. Normally
one checks $\mathbf 1^{\!\top}M^{m}w=0$ for $m<S$ and appeals to Cayley--Hamilton for the
rest; here $w$ itself vanishes, so $p$ annihilates $\mathbf u$ under $M$ outright and no
appeal is needed. (The weaker check was run as well, for $m=0,\dots,41$, and every value
is $0$.) Solving for the recurrence is only a way of \emph{proposing} \eqref{eq:true};
what makes it a theorem is this annihilation, which is independent of how the candidate
was found.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}
\eqref{eq:conj} fails for every $n\ge11$, not merely at the ten indices the entry's DATA
exhibits.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Subtracting \eqref{eq:conj} from \eqref{eq:true}, a failure of \eqref{eq:conj} at $n$
amounts to
$583350\,a(n-11)+364874\,a(n-12)+80630\,a(n-13)\ne0$ for $n\ge14$; every $a(m)$ is a
count and $a(m)\ge6>0$ for all $m\ge1$, and all three coefficients are positive, so the
left side is positive. For $n=11,12,13$ the table in Section~2 exhibits the failure
directly.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification}

Five checks, each independent of the others.

First, the disproof in Section~2 uses nothing but the entry's own published integers and
ten multiply--adds per index. It does not depend on the model, on the b-file, or on
Theorem~\ref{thm:true}.

Second, the model was not taken on trust. Its first seven values were confirmed by
brute-force enumeration over all $6^{n}$ tuples, it reproduces all $20$ published DATA
terms, and it reproduces all $210$ terms of the entry's b-file --- every one, not a
sample.

Third, \eqref{eq:true} was checked directly on $260$ exactly computed model terms, and
holds at all $247$ indices where it asserts anything, before any matrix argument was
made. Orders $1$ through $12$ were each solved for and each fails, so $13$ is minimal.

Fourth, the annihilation test of Theorem~\ref{thm:true} was carried out in exact integer
arithmetic and gave the zero vector, and the weaker Krylov test was carried to the full
Cayley--Hamilton bound $m<42$ rather than to a sampled prefix.

Fifth, the entry's parent table A269640 independently records the order of this column
as $13$: its ``Empirical for column k'' block prints the recurrences for $k=1,2,3$ and
then, for $k=4,\dots,7$, only the placeholders ``[order 7]'', ``[order 13]'', ``[order
14]'', ``[order 16]''. Column $k=5$ is A269637, and the order named there is $13$, not
$10$.

\begin{remark}
The shape of the error decides what this note is. The published coefficients are not
wrong; they are incomplete, and the line is the correct recurrence truncated after ten
terms. What distinguishes this case from an over-fitted empirical recurrence reported at
an order its sample cannot support is that the truncation is visible from the entry
alone: the line contradicts the data printed a few lines above it, at ten consecutive
indices, and the parent table names the right order. The correction is \eqref{eq:true}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
The other columns of A269640 were checked the same way. The empirical lines for
A269635 ($k=3$, order 7), A269636 ($k=4$, order 7), A269638 ($k=6$, order 14) and
A269639 ($k=7$, order 16) hold at every index their published DATA can test. A269643 and
A269644 carry lines whose stated range ``for $n>9$'' and ``for $n>10$'' excludes the only
indices at which they disagree with the data, so they too are untouched by this note.
A269637 is the only member of the family whose published line is false.
\end{remark}
