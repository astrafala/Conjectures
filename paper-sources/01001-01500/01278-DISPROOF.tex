\title{The empirical recurrence for OEIS A236647 is false at every index its b-file can
test, and the correct one, of order 38}
\author{Adrian Perez Fontelles\\ \small Independent researcher}
\date{22 September 2026}
\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
OEIS A236647 carries an empirical recurrence of order $34$ contributed by R.~H.~Hardin. It
is false. The entry's own b-file runs to $n=210$, so the recurrence can be tested at $176$
indices, and it fails at all $176$; the first is $n=35$, where the recurrence falls short of
the b-file's $36$-digit value by $145084608$. A
transfer matrix on $224$ states, brute-force confirmed and reproducing all $210$ b-file
terms, gives the recurrence that does hold: it has order $38$, its first $34$
coefficients are exactly the published ones, and it holds for every $n\ge40$, failing at
$n=39$ and there only. The published line is the correct recurrence with its last four
terms dropped, and the entry's own parent table A236651 already says so, recording this
column as ``[order 38] for n>39''.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\small 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 05A15, 05B45, 15B36.\normalsize

\section{The sequence and the claim}

OEIS A236647 is ``Number of (n+1)X(2+1) 0..2 arrays colored with the maximum plus the upper
median minus the lower median minus the minimum of every 2X2 subblock.''. It has offset $1$,
publishes $16$ terms in DATA and a b-file of $210$. It carries one formula line, an
empirical recurrence of order $34$:
\begin{multline}\label{eq:conj}
a(n)\;=\;13\,a(n-2) + 184\,a(n-3) + 3506\,a(n-4) + 9110\,a(n-5)\\
{}+ 21587\,a(n-6) + 63605\,a(n-7) - 342128\,a(n-8) - 864643\,a(n-9)\\
{}+ 1140624\,a(n-10) + 294974\,a(n-11) + 739053\,a(n-12) + 15509266\,a(n-13)\\
{}- 16660349\,a(n-14) - 37563062\,a(n-15) + 41074149\,a(n-16) - 39645704\,a(n-17)\\
{}- 48556860\,a(n-18) + 140778823\,a(n-19) - 72899036\,a(n-20) - 67241570\,a(n-21)\\
{}+ 329687733\,a(n-22) - 46469782\,a(n-23) - 133466871\,a(n-24) + 319656720\,a(n-25)\\
{}- 408928291\,a(n-26) - 40519358\,a(n-27) + 151099557\,a(n-28) - 160188966\,a(n-29)\\
{}+ 104521624\,a(n-30) - 63973040\,a(n-31) - 163603316\,a(n-32) + 759648\,a(n-33)\\
{}+ 12746672\,a(n-34).
\end{multline}
As of the live entry (revision $6$, last modified Jul 23 2025) this is recorded as empirical,
with no range stated and nothing recording it as settled or corrected.

With offset $1$ and order $34$ the line first asserts something at $n=35$. DATA stops at
$n=16$, so on the DATA alone the claim is untestable --- but the entry links a b-file running
to $n=210$, which tests it $176$ times.

\section{The published recurrence fails at all 176 indices its b-file can test}

Substituting the b-file's own integers into the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:conj}:

\begin{tabular}{rr}
\toprule
$n$ & $a(n)-\text{RHS of \eqref{eq:conj}}$ \\
\midrule
$35$ & $145084608$\\
$36$ & $1515557632$\\
$37$ & $14366219776$\\

$40$ & $11713661278720$\\

\begin{proposition}
\eqref{eq:conj} is false. It fails at every one of the $176$ indices
$n=35,\dots,210$ at which the entry's b-file can test it. The smallest is $n=35$, where
\[
a(35)\;=\;470259440232820855339223326337397112
\]
and the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:conj} gives
\[
470259440232820855339223326192312504,
\]
a difference of $145084608$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Direct evaluation in exact integer arithmetic on the $210$ integers the entry's b-file
publishes. No model enters this computation.
\end{proof}

\section{What the entry counts}

The wording is Hardin's and is terse, so it is worth fixing precisely, and then checking the
reading rather than assuming it. Let $A$ be an $(n+1)\times3$ array over $\{0,1,2\}$. Each
$2\times2$ subblock has four entries; sorted as $w\le x\le y\le z$, give the subblock the
\emph{colour}
\[
\chi \;=\; z + y - x - w ,
\]
the maximum plus the upper median minus the lower median minus the minimum. The subblocks of
$A$ form an $n\times2$ grid, and the condition is that \emph{horizontally and vertically
adjacent subblocks get different colours} --- the array is properly coloured by $\chi$.
Let $a(n)$ count such arrays.

For $n=1$ there are two subblocks, side by side, and the condition is one inequality; for
$n=2$ there are four, in a $2\times2$ grid. Direct enumeration over all $3^{3(n+1)}$ arrays
gives
\[
484,\quad 4500,\quad 41980 \qquad (n=1,2,3),
\]
the entry's first three terms exactly.

\section{The transfer matrix, and the recurrence that does hold}

A row of the array is one of $3^{3}=27$ tuples, and whether a new row may be added depends on
the previous row and on the colours of the subblock row just completed --- on nothing else.
Take as vertices the pairs (last row, colour pair), keep those reachable, and there are
$S=224$ of them; let $M$ be the adjacency matrix of the legal extensions and $\mathbf u$ the
distribution after the first two rows. Then $a(n)=\mathbf1^{\!\top}M^{\,n-1}\mathbf u$ for
$n\ge1$. Evaluated exactly, this reproduces all $16$ published DATA terms and all $210$
b-file terms, and its first three values are the brute-force counts above.

Being a walk count, $a$ is $C$-finite. Solving over $\mathbf Q$ from the model's terms, the
recurrence that holds on the tail has order $38$ with integer coefficients:
\begin{multline}\label{eq:true}
a(n)\;=\;13\,a(n-2) + 184\,a(n-3) + 3506\,a(n-4) + 9110\,a(n-5)\\
{}+ 21587\,a(n-6) + 63605\,a(n-7) - 342128\,a(n-8) - 864643\,a(n-9)\\
{}+ 1140624\,a(n-10) + 294974\,a(n-11) + 739053\,a(n-12) + 15509266\,a(n-13)\\
{}- 16660349\,a(n-14) - 37563062\,a(n-15) + 41074149\,a(n-16) - 39645704\,a(n-17)\\
{}- 48556860\,a(n-18) + 140778823\,a(n-19) - 72899036\,a(n-20) - 67241570\,a(n-21)\\
{}+ 329687733\,a(n-22) - 46469782\,a(n-23) - 133466871\,a(n-24) + 319656720\,a(n-25)\\
{}- 408928291\,a(n-26) - 40519358\,a(n-27) + 151099557\,a(n-28) - 160188966\,a(n-29)\\
{}+ 104521624\,a(n-30) - 63973040\,a(n-31) - 163603316\,a(n-32) + 759648\,a(n-33)\\
{}+ 12746672\,a(n-34)\\
{}1733120\,a(n-35) + 12956032\,a(n-36) + 5889024\,a(n-37) - 243712\,a(n-38).
\end{multline}
Its first $34$ coefficients are, term for term, those of \eqref{eq:conj}. The published line
is \eqref{eq:true} with its last four terms,
$+1733120\,a(n-35)+12956032\,a(n-36)+5889024\,a(n-37)-243712\,a(n-38)$, missing.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:true}
\eqref{eq:true} holds for every $n\ge40$, and fails at $n=39$, where the two sides differ by
$114688$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $p(t)$ be the characteristic polynomial of \eqref{eq:true},
\begin{multline*}
p(t)\;=\;t^{38} - 13t^{36} - 184t^{35} - 3506t^{34} - 9110t^{33} - 21587t^{32}\\
{}- 63605t^{31} + 342128t^{30} + 864643t^{29} - 1140624t^{28} - 294974t^{27} - 739053t^{26}\\
{}- 15509266t^{25} + 16660349t^{24} + 37563062t^{23} - 41074149t^{22} + 39645704t^{21} + 48556860t^{20}\\
{}- 140778823t^{19} + 72899036t^{18} + 67241570t^{17} - 329687733t^{16} + 46469782t^{15} + 133466871t^{14}\\
{}- 319656720t^{13} + 408928291t^{12} + 40519358t^{11} - 151099557t^{10} + 160188966t^{9} - 104521624t^{8}\\
{}+ 63973040t^{7} + 163603316t^{6} - 759648t^{5} - 12746672t^{4} - 1733120t^{3} - 12956032t^{2}\\
{}- 5889024t + 243712,
\end{multline*}
and put $w=p(M)\mathbf u$, formed by $38$ matrix--vector products in exact integer
arithmetic. For $n\ge39$,
\[
a(n)-\sum_{i=1}^{38}c_i\,a(n-i)\;=\;\mathbf1^{\!\top}M^{\,n-39}w .
\]
At $n=39$ this is $\mathbf1^{\!\top}w=114688\ne0$, which is the stated exception. For
$n\ge40$ it is $\mathbf1^{\!\top}M^{\,m}(Mw)$ with $m=n-40\ge0$, and that vanishes for every
$m=0,\dots,S$; by the Cayley--Hamilton theorem $M^{S}$ is an integer combination of
$I,M,\dots,M^{S-1}$, so it vanishes for every $m\ge0$.
\end{proof}

Solving for the recurrence only \emph{proposes} \eqref{eq:true}; what makes it a theorem is
the annihilation test, which is independent of how the candidate was found.

\begin{corollary}
\eqref{eq:conj} fails for every $n\ge35$, not merely at the $176$ indices the b-file
exhibits.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For $n\ge40$, subtracting \eqref{eq:conj} from \eqref{eq:true} makes a failure of
\eqref{eq:conj} at $n$ equivalent to
\[
1733120\,a(n-35)+12956032\,a(n-36)+5889024\,a(n-37)-243712\,a(n-38)\;\ne\;0 .
\]
Every state of the transfer graph has out-degree at least $5$, checked over all $224$ of
them, so every admissible array of $n+1$ rows extends to one of $n+2$ rows and $a$ is
nondecreasing; every $a(m)$ is a count of a nonempty set, so $a(m)\ge a(1)=484>0$. Hence
each of $a(n-35),a(n-36),a(n-37)$ is at least $a(n-38)$ and the expression is at least
$(1733120+12956032+5889024-243712)\,a(n-38)>0$. For $35\le n\le39$ the table in Section~2
exhibits the failure directly.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification}

Five checks.

First, the disproof in Section~2 uses only the $210$ integers of the entry's own b-file. It
does not depend on the model or on Theorem~\ref{thm:true}.

Second, the reading of the entry's wording was not assumed. It was confirmed by direct
enumeration over all $3^{3(n+1)}$ arrays for $n=1,2,3$, which gives $484$, $4500$, $41980$
--- the entry's first three terms. The same rule, applied at width $2$ instead of $3$,
reproduces the first four terms of A236646, the $k=1$ column of the same table.

Third, the transfer model reproduces all $16$ published DATA terms and all $210$ b-file
terms, every one and not a sample.

Fourth, \eqref{eq:true} was checked directly against the b-file at every index from $n=39$
to $n=210$: it fails at $n=39$ and holds at all $171$ others, matching
Theorem~\ref{thm:true} exactly. Every order $1,\dots,37$ was solved for on the tail
(fitted at $n=140$) and every one fails there --- order $37$ at $22$ of the remaining $61$
indices, and the failure count rises steadily as the order falls --- so $38$ is the least
order that holds eventually, and \eqref{eq:conj}'s $34$ is not merely incomplete
bookkeeping but too short to be repaired by any choice of coefficients.

Fifth, the annihilation test was carried to the full Cayley--Hamilton bound $m\le S=224$,
not to a sampled prefix.

\begin{remark}
The entry's parent table already carries the right answer. A236651 is the table of which
A236647 is column $2$, and its ``Empirical for column k'' block records
\begin{quote}\small
k=1: [linear recurrence of order 11]\\
k=2: [order 38] for n>39
\end{quote}
--- naming both the order, $38$, and the exception, $n>39$, that
Theorem~\ref{thm:true} proves. The column entry prints an order-$34$ line instead. Two
statements on the OEIS about the same recurrence disagree, and the table is the one that is
right.

This is the second instance of the same shape found by the same scan. The other is A269637,
column $5$ of A269640, where the table says ``[order 13]'' and the column entry publishes an
order-$10$ line that its own DATA refutes ten times over. Across $6430$ table/column pairs in
the OEIS these two are the only disagreements, and both are the column entry's line, truncated.
\end{remark}
