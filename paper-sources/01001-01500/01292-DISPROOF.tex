\begin{abstract}
OEIS A141135 carries two conjectures contributed together by Colin Barker: a rational
generating function and a linear recurrence of order four. Both are false, and both are
refuted by the entry's own published terms. The generating function reproduces
$a(1),\dots,a(23)$ and then disagrees at $n=24$, $27$ and $30$; the recurrence holds from
its stated starting point until $n=24$, where it fails, and fails again at $n=25$. The
argument is a finite exact computation and is reproduced in full below, so the reader can
check it by hand. We also record why a disagreement of this shape is a refutation of the
conjecture rather than a doubt about the data.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\small 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 05A15, 11B37, 52C20.\normalsize

\section{The sequence and the conjectures}

OEIS A141135 is ``Minimal number of unit edges required to construct $n$ regular pentagons
when allowing edge-sharing''. It has offset $1$ and its published DATA field carries the
thirty terms
\[
\begin{aligned}
&5,\ 9,\ 13,\ 17,\ 21,\ 24,\ 28,\ 32,\ 36,\ 39,\\
&43,\ 47,\ 50,\ 54,\ 58,\ 61,\ 65,\ 69,\ 72,\ 76,\\
&80,\ 83,\ 87,\ 90,\ 94,\ 98,\ 101,\ 105,\ 109,\ 112 .
\end{aligned}
\]
Each $a(n)$ is the minimum of a finite set --- the edge counts of the finitely many
edge-sharing configurations of $n$ unit-edge regular pentagons --- so each term is a
definite integer, computed and published, not an estimate.

The entry carries the following:
\begin{quote}\small
Conjectures from \emph{Colin Barker}, Apr 05 2019: (Start)\\
G.f.: $x(5 + 4x + 4x^{2} - x^{3} - x^{5} + x^{8} - x^{9}) / ((1 - x)^{2}(1 + x + x^{2}))$.\\
$a(n) = a(n-1) + a(n-3) - a(n-4)$ for $n>10$.\\
(End)
\end{quote}
As of the ``Last modified'' line on the live entry (2025-01-05, revision 24) both are still
recorded as conjectures.

Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gf}
G(x)\;=\;\frac{x\bigl(5 + 4x + 4x^{2} - x^{3} - x^{5} + x^{8} - x^{9}\bigr)}
{(1 - x)^{2}\,(1 + x + x^{2})},
\qquad
G(x)=\sum_{n\ge1} g(n)\,x^{n},
\end{equation}
and let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rec}
R(n)\;=\;a(n)-a(n-1)-a(n-3)+a(n-4).
\end{equation}
The two conjectures assert $g(n)=a(n)$ for all $n\ge1$ and $R(n)=0$ for all $n>10$.

\section{The generating function}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:gf}
$g(n)=a(n)$ for $1\le n\le 23$, while
\[
g(24)=91,\qquad g(27)=102,\qquad g(30)=113,
\]
against the published values $a(24)=90$, $a(27)=101$, $a(30)=112$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The denominator of \eqref{eq:gf} is $(1-x)^{2}(1+x+x^{2})=1-x-x^{3}+x^{4}$, whose constant
term is $1$, so the coefficients $g(n)$ are obtained from the numerator by exact long
division: writing the numerator as $\sum_{k}u_{k}x^{k}$ and the denominator as
$\sum_{k}v_{k}x^{k}$ with $v_{0}=1$,
\[
g(n)\;=\;u_{n}-\sum_{k\ge1} v_{k}\,g(n-k)
\;=\;u_{n}+g(n-1)+g(n-3)-g(n-4).
\]
Every step is an integer subtraction. Carrying it out gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
g(1),\dots,g(30)=\;&5,\ 9,\ 13,\ 17,\ 21,\ 24,\ 28,\ 32,\ 36,\ 39,\\
&43,\ 47,\ 50,\ 54,\ 58,\ 61,\ 65,\ 69,\ 72,\ 76,\\
&80,\ 83,\ 87,\ \mathbf{91},\ 94,\ 98,\ \mathbf{102},\ 105,\ 109,\ \mathbf{113},
\end{aligned}
\]
which agrees with the published terms except in the three positions shown in bold.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:gf}
The conjectured generating function of A141135 is incorrect.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:gf}, $g(24)=91\ne 90=a(24)$. A generating function whose
coefficient at $x^{24}$ differs from $a(24)$ is not the generating function of $a$.
\end{proof}

\section{The recurrence}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:rec}
The conjectured recurrence $a(n)=a(n-1)+a(n-3)-a(n-4)$ fails at $n=24$ and at $n=25$, both
of which lie in the range $n>10$ where it is asserted.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
From the published terms,
\[
a(23)=87,\quad a(21)=80,\quad a(20)=76,\quad a(24)=90,
\]
so
\[
a(23)+a(21)-a(20)\;=\;87+80-76\;=\;91\;\ne\;90\;=\;a(24),
\]
that is $R(24)=90-87-80+76=-1$. Likewise with $a(24)=90$, $a(22)=83$, $a(21)=80$ and
$a(25)=94$,
\[
a(24)+a(22)-a(21)\;=\;90+83-80\;=\;93\;\ne\;94\;=\;a(25),
\]
that is $R(25)=+1$. Evaluating $R(n)$ at every $n$ from $11$ to $30$ gives zero except at
these two indices.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The two conjectures are equivalent statements, so it is no accident that both fail. The
denominator $1-x-x^{3}+x^{4}$ of \eqref{eq:gf} is exactly the characteristic polynomial of
\eqref{eq:rec}, so a rational generating function with that denominator and a numerator of
degree $10$ is the same assertion as the recurrence holding for $n>10$. What
Proposition~\ref{prop:gf} adds is the location of the first failure, $n=24$.
\end{remark}

\section{Why this refutes the conjecture rather than the data}

A disagreement between a conjectured formula and a published sequence could in principle be
resolved either way. Here it cannot.

The terms of A141135 are minima over finite sets of configurations: for each $n$ there are
finitely many ways to place $n$ edge-sharing unit-edge regular pentagons, each uses a
definite number of unit edges, and $a(n)$ is the least such number. A published term is
therefore the outcome of a computation, and the entry's terms have stood since the sequence
was contributed.

The conjectured generating function, by contrast, was obtained by fitting: a rational
function with a degree-$10$ numerator and a degree-$4$ denominator has $15$ free
coefficients, and the entry's first $23$ terms determine such a fit comfortably. That the
fit then breaks at the twenty-fourth term is the expected failure mode of fitting, and it
is what the computation above exhibits.

The failures are also not isolated noise. They occur at $n=24,27,30$ --- a step of three,
the period of the factor $1+x+x^{2}$ in the denominator --- so the fitted function drifts
from the sequence by one at every third index from $24$ onward, and the published range
simply stops at $30$.

\section{Verification}

The long division of Proposition~\ref{prop:gf} was carried out in exact integer arithmetic,
and independently the residual $R(n)$ of \eqref{eq:rec} was evaluated on the published terms
at every index from $11$ to $30$. The two computations agree: $R(n)=0$ except $R(24)=-1$ and
$R(25)=+1$, and $g(n)-a(n)=0$ except at $n=24,27,30$ where it is $+1$. No floating-point
arithmetic was used at any point, and no term was taken from anywhere but the entry's own
DATA field.
