## A352117

OEIS entry: <https://oeis.org/A352117>  
Result obtained: **25 August 2026**  
Paper: [15](../papers/00001-00500/00015-PROOF.pdf)

What the entry records as unsettled:

> Conjectural o.g.f. as a continued fraction of Stieltjes type: 1/(1 - x/(1 - 4*x/(1 - 3*x/(1 - 8*x/(1 - ... - (2*n-1)*x/(1 - 4*n*x/(1 - ... ))))))). Cf. A346982. - _Peter Bala_, Aug 22 2023
> Conjecture: a(n) = Sum_{k>=1} 2^(n - 3*k - 1/2) * k^n * binomial(2*k, k) for n > 0. - _Velin Yanev_ and _Vaclav Kotesovec_, Jul 21 2026

Proposed OEIS comment:

```
This is true for n > 0. Forming the exponential generating function of the right-hand side and exchanging the order of summation turns the inner sum into the central binomial series (1 - 4z)^(-1/2) at z = e^(2x)/8, and 1 - 4*e^(2x)/8 cancels against 2 - e^(2x). What is left over is the k = 0 term, which the summation range excludes, contributing the constant 1/sqrt(2); that affects only the coefficient of x^0, which is exactly why the conjecture has to be stated for n > 0. - _Adrian Perez Fontelles_, Sep 06 2026
```

## A354242

OEIS entry: <https://oeis.org/A354242>  
Result obtained: **25 August 2026**  
Paper: [96](../papers/00001-00500/00096-PROOF.pdf)

What the entry records as unsettled:

> Conjecture: Let k be a positive integer. The sequence obtained by reducing a(n) modulo k is eventually periodic with the period dividing phi(k) = A000010(k). For example, modulo 16 we obtain the sequence [1, 2, 14, 14, 6, 14, 6, 14, 6, ...], with an apparent period of 2 beginning at a(3). Cf. A354253.
> More generally, we conjecture that the same property holds for integer sequences having an e.g.f. of the form G(exp(x) - 1), where G(x) is an integral power series. (End)
> Conjectural o.g.f. as a continued fraction of Stieltjes type: 1/(1 - 2*x/(1 - 5*x/(1 - 6*x/(1 - 10*x/(1 - 10*x/(1 - 15*x/(1 - ... - (4*n-2)*x/(1 - 5*n*x/(1 - ...))))))))). - _Peter Bala_, Jul 07 2022

Proposed OEIS comment:

```
This is true. Under t = e^x - 1 the entry's e.g.f. becomes an ordinary power series with integer coefficients; that integrality kills all but finitely many terms of the Stirling expansion of a(n) modulo k, and what is left is an integer combination of the sequences n -> j^n, each eventually periodic mod k with period dividing phi(k). - _Adrian Perez Fontelles_, Sep 06 2026
```

## A358272

OEIS entry: <https://oeis.org/A358272>  
Result obtained: **25 August 2026**  
Paper: [25](../papers/00001-00500/00025-PROOF.pdf)

What the entry records as unsettled:

> Conjecture: a(n) = Sum_{k=1..n} gcd(k, n) * lambda(gcd(k, n)) for n > 0.

Proposed OEIS comment:

```
This is true, and elementary. Group the k in {1, ..., n} by the value of gcd(k,n): any sum of that shape becomes a Dirichlet convolution with Euler's totient, and evaluating the resulting local factor at a prime power is a two-line alternating sum that collapses according to the parity of the exponent. - _Adrian Perez Fontelles_, Sep 06 2026
```
